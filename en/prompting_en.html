Semantic Enrichment through Large Language Models (LLMs)

In this phase of the project, we moved away from the SPARQL CONSTRUCT approach to delegate the generation of new knowledge to Large Language Models. The goal was to act as prompt engineering experts to instruct the AI to extract information from biographical sources and translate it into the formal language of the Semantic Web: the Turtle format.

Methodology and Prompting Strategies

To ensure the accuracy of results, we applied three different prompting techniques, testing the model’s reasoning ability across increasing levels of complexity:

  • Zero-Shot Prompting: We provided direct instructions without examples, relying on the model’s pre-trained knowledge of the Wikidata ontology;
  • Few-Shot Prompting: We guided the model by providing 2 to 5 input-output examples, establishing a consistent schema for triple generation;
  • Chain-of-Thought (CoT): We encouraged the model to explicitly show its step-by-step reasoning (e.g., “Let’s think step by step”) before producing the final code. This technique proved essential for resolving logical ambiguities between occupations and fields of activity, for example.
  • Few-Shot Chain-of-Thought (FS-CoT): We combined input-output examples with step-by-step reasoning examples, showing the model both how to structure RDF triples and the logical process to derive them. This approach improved response consistency and handling of complex or ambiguous cases.

Comparative Analysis of Models

The work included a comparison between different language models, such as GPT-4 and Claude Sonnet 4.6. This comparative analysis allowed us to evaluate:

  • Turtle code quality: Syntactic correctness and use of proper prefixes (wd:, wdt:);
  • Semantic accuracy: The ability to distinguish between closely related concepts (e.g., the difference between "Occupation" - P106 and "Field of work" - P101);
  • Hallucinations: The tendency of models to invent QIDs or non-existent properties, requiring strict manual verification of outputs.
