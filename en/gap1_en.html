Gap 1: Institutional Status and Historical Primacy

Laura Bassi was the first woman in Europe to obtain an official academic position in Physics at the University of Bologna. However, on Wikidata this fact is not modeled as an explicit property but is only “implicit” in the combination of:

  • "occupation" (P106) = physicist
  • "employer" (P108) = University of Bologna

The property “position held” (P39) is not used, even though it would explicitly represent the institutional office assigned to her by official decree, strengthening the semantic representation of her historical achievement.

The semantic gap lies precisely here: although biographical sources emphasize Laura Bassi's pioneering role as the first woman in Europe to hold a university chair in Physics, this information is not explicitly modeled in the knowledge graph as a structured relationship, but instead remains implicit through the combination of occupation and academic affiliation.

"Occupation" vs. "Position held"

Laura Bassi's historical achievement is not modeled as an institutional office (P39 — missing), but simply as an occupation. We also discovered the existence of item Q107609509 ("first woman to hold a position"), which is a Wikidata item rather than a property. This reveals not only a quantitative gap (missing data), but also a structural one: Wikidata currently lacks an appropriate vocabulary for representing historical firsts of this kind. 

SELECT DISTINCT ?person ?personLabel ?occupation ?occupationLabel 
                ?position ?positionLabel
WHERE {
  VALUES ?person { wd:Q541652 } # The ?person variable is strictly Laura Bassi
  
  OPTIONAL { wd:Q541652 wdt:P106 ?occupation }     # occupation
  OPTIONAL { wd:Q541652 wdt:P39 ?position }        # position held
  
  SERVICE wikibase:label { bd:serviceParam wikibase:language "it,en". }
}
